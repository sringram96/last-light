# Architecture

The game runs entirely in the browser. `scripts/build.mjs` inserts the new-material extension into the approved renderer and assembles the runtime in dependency order. `dist/index.html` embeds the result and is playable without network access.

## Rendering

The scene builders create 3D surfaces and lights. The camera transforms and projects them; clipped polygons become depth-tested ASCII cells. Surface materials derive glyphs and colors from world coordinates. Camera movement and actor blocking are tied to story phases.

Characters are text sprite sheets (`src/game/sprites.js`, with the design team's sheets inlined from `docs/design/sprites.json` at build time). Each character has `full`, `mid` and `small` poses; `actor()` in `runtime.js` snaps the figure to whole cells, picks the sheet that fits the projected size without squeezing it below 0.7, and draws each sheet cell into its band of screen cells so that fills tile, outlines and faces are drawn once, and enclosed spaces stay opaque. Walk frames advance with distance travelled. A blocking entry names its sheet with `who` and its pose with `pose`; `docs/design/TECH.md` has the rules and the loader API.


An `examine` phase (`src/game/examine.js`) is an object turned over in Rook's hands. The picture cuts to an insert: the object is modelled at hand scale far below every set's floor, inside a room whose walls face inward and draw nothing but dust (the `void` material), so the set around it never shows; blocking, rain and the set's labels are skipped while it is on. The camera circles the object as the player turns it: a drag on the picture, the arrow keys or WASD (an eighth of a turn round, a sixth up or down), or `[<< TURN]`, `[TURN >>]` and `[TIP IT]` (underside, top, level) in the strip; the turn eases, and lands at once under reduced motion. A detail is an engraving on one face (`lines` at `at`, facing `normal`): it is drawn, with its examine marker beside it, only while that face is within about sixty degrees of the camera, and only then can it be read by its marker, its number key or its button. Reading one saves its bit in the phase's field (`lanternLooked`, `padlockLooked`), adds its clue once and replaces the caption; a detail once read keeps its button. `[PUT IT DOWN]` enters `back` and cuts the camera straight to that beat's shot. A look-around spot with `pick` offers its object while the spot is selected. The route line comes from the bitmask like a look-around's.

## Frame budget

The loop draws as often as the last frame's cost allows: the gap between draws is about 1.7 times the measured render, floored at a thirtieth of a second and capped at a tenth, so a cheap picture runs at thirty a second and an expensive one backs off rather than saturating the thread. A glyph row is drawn as runs of equal ink instead of one canvas call per cell, which is most of what a frame costs. A set may ask for a denser grid (the menu's `density`); a device whose frames run over budget three times running has that density eased a step, so a phone draws the menu at the story's grid while a desktop keeps the dense one. The first dozen frames are not judged, because a cold start pays for fonts, layout and compilation once, and a machine that then draws ninety frames inside half its budget earns a step back, so an opening hitch does not cost the picture for the rest of the session. Story beats are held to a thirty-a-second budget because they are played; the menu, which only drifts, is allowed a cinematic twenty-five.

## Story

`case.js` owns the case phases and branch decisions. `runtime.js` retains the approved opening and calls the case code for later locations. A phase starts with `enter()`, updates the visible choices, and records a checkpoint. Timed movement and quick-time prompts advance through the animation loop. Quiet choices wait for the player.

Newer sets live in `src/game/sets/`, one file each, and register themselves through `registry.js`: `registerSet()` takes the set's chapter card, objective, geometry, camera shots, blocking, labels, exit point and preview, and `registerPhases()` takes its beats as data (kind, duration, caption, buttons, moves, clues, rewind entry and successor). `case.js`, `scenes.js`, `presentation.js` and `runtime.js` consult the registries first and fall back to the hand-written sets. The build concatenates the set files after `scenes.js` and before `case.js`.

An `investigate` phase is a quiet look around a set: `spots` name world points with a label, a caption, an optional camera, clue and `enter()`, and the phase's `field` is a saved bitmask of the spots examined (`officeLooked` and the other `*Looked` fields, five bits each), which `enter()` never resets, so a resume keeps them. Each visible spot draws an examine marker (`[n]`, dim amber until examined, dim cyan after, steady in every mode) through `worldLabel()` ahead of every sprite, so it records a tap rectangle like a cue; a tap on the marker, its number key or its button under the picture calls `investigate()`, which sets the bit, runs the clue and `enter()` once, plays the spot's caption through the caption queue (an examined spot re-reads for nothing), restarts the phase clock and eases the camera to the spot's shot (`caseShot()` prefers the selected spot's camera; `investigateSpot()` tells blocking and labels which one is selected). A spot with `after` waits for another spot's bit; the exit button shows once `need` visible spots are examined, an `early` exit while its `when()` holds; there is no deadline, arrows do nothing, and the status line reads `LOOK AROUND` then `YOUR MOVE`. A beat with `turns` has time for that many first looks: the title counts them down, an optional `pressure()` line follows each look's caption, and once they are spent the unexamined spots close in the buttons, the number keys and the picture (`spotOpen()`), leaving the examined ones to re-read and the exit. The route line comes from the bitmask (`step` and the count). The office desk (`officeDesk`, four spots, two before the stairs) is the first use; checkpoints at the removed `officeFile`, `officeBoard` and `officeWindow` resume there.

Fourteen sets exist: office, street, loft, station, pump room, roof, tram, market, club, elevated road, undercity, substation, interview room and canal. The office is an arrival cutscene and the desk look-around, then the street. The street deduction can detour through Bell's loft. From the roof, pursuing Vale goes by the tram, the market and the club to the road; the lower ramp at the bridge leads into the undercity, whose fork decides whether Vale is caught. Both chases end at Substation Nine, then the interview room and the canal. Staying with Bell goes by the tram and the station straight to the interview room. Each set is built once and cached; the road, the undercity and the tram move with the pursuit distance.

The derived helpers in `registry.js` (`ledgerHeld`, `manifestHeld`, `chipHeld`, `proofHeld`, `kranePinned`, `pursuing`) read only checkpoint fields, so a phase never stores a conclusion it can compute.

## Transitions and rewinds

`enter()` defers a phase whose set differs from the current one: it stores the pending phase, an exit shot derived from the current camera and the set's exit point, and a connecting caption, then the animation loop glides the camera, drives a fade value to zero and only then applies the phase. The fade dims palette levels and thins glyphs in `render()` after the frame is composed, so it never touches the scene builders. Menus and pauses freeze a transition in place. Direct entries (resume, previews, reduced motion) skip it.

Action beats are prompts with two directional cues placed at world points. `promptInput(dir, event)` in `case.js` is the one entry for arrow keys, WASD, a swipe on the canvas and a tap on a cue's hit rectangle (recorded by `worldLabel()` each frame, like the sprite rectangles); a matching cue runs the move, and a direction the beat does not offer is ignored, so a stray swipe or a mashed key is never a move; only the window running out is the miss. The cue is drawn as its arrows inside a solid frame that stays lit between flashes, drawn ahead of everything, and its tap rectangle is the frame. The strip names the two moves as buttons pointing the way their cues do (a button is a tap on its cue, timed from its click), and the status line's `LIVE` carries a bar that drains with the window. Windows are 1.25 to 3.5 s from `caseDuration()`; during a prompt the frame clock is capped at 0.05 s so a hitch cannot hand out time, and pauses, menus and background tabs freeze it as before.

Lethal misses enter a `death` phase (registry kind `death`): a lamp (`state.rewinds`) is spent, the death bit is added to `state.deaths`, the picture and caption play, and the phase's `back` windup re-enters with its `reset()` applied and the rewind line as its caption. With no lamp left the death leads to `coldCase`, a set-less phase that holds the death frame, writes the `cold` ending, the cold count and the deaths seen to the records, and offers a chapter restart (three lamps, `restarts` +1, the set's entry phase, only that set's fields reset) or the menu. Survivable misses are result phases with a worse story and a rewind that costs a lamp.

## Presentation

`presentation.js` owns everything that makes a beat feel like a game without touching the scene: a five-row block font for chapter cards and stingers, the typed narration, the case file (route, persons of interest, evidence), the reflex tally and the case records. `enter()` reports each phase change and whether the scene changed; the presentation layer decides whether that is a chapter card, a landed or missed stinger, a warning, or nothing. Everything it draws is HTML beside the canvas, in a fixed-height strip under the picture (or beside it on a landscape phone); nothing is ever positioned over the canvas, and the picture is fitted to the viewport by a vertical field-of-view rule that keeps 70 rows and adds columns. Captions are split into sentence chunks that type at 45 characters per second and hold until they can be read; a cutscene or result ends no earlier than its caption (`holdFor()`), and a tap advances a chunk without ending the beat. Once a cutscene's chunks have all been read (`captionDone()`) and its authored duration has elapsed, a tap on the picture or the caption, Enter or Space ends it (`endCutscene()` in `case.js`, which reads registered cutscenes by kind and the hand-written case and street ones from `cutsceneEnds` and `streetCutscenes`); results, windups, prompts, deaths and observes keep their own clocks. `audio.js` synthesizes short cues from oscillators when the sound preference is on; it has no assets and is silent by default.

Narration types in from a copy of the caption. The visible caption is hidden from assistive technology and a visually hidden live region receives the full sentence once, so screen readers hear whole sentences rather than fragments.

## Sessions and persistence

`session.js` distinguishes the main menu, story play, scene previews and the idle tableau (`showIdle()`: the `menu` set and its `menuIdle` phase, shown at boot and after a closed or cold case; the story clock and the camera run while the menu is open, and nothing checkpoints). Previews can run every location but never write the story checkpoint. Opening the menu freezes the phase and its deadline. Starting over requires a confirmation when a checkpoint exists.

`save-store.js` validates known phase names, field types, numeric bounds and clue strings before loading. Saves use a versioned envelope under `last-light/save/v1`. Existing prototype saves from `the-last-light-case-v2` migrate. Settings are stored independently under `last-light/settings/v1` and survive new cases. Case records (endings reached, discoveries made, cases closed) live under `last-light/records/v1`; they are written only when a story session, never a preview, reaches the canal ending, and a new case does not clear them. Storage failures leave a session-only checkpoint and do not prevent play.

Endings, discoveries, the route and the reflex score are all derived from checkpoint fields. Fields added for the longer case (`note`, `loftSeen`, `misread`, `tail`, `keeper`, `market`, `hall`, `slip`) and the look-around bitmasks (`officeLooked`, `stationLooked`, `pumpLooked`, `loftLooked`, `subLooked`, `clubLooked`, integers 0..31) are validated like the originals; a checkpoint saved at the removed `ending` phase resumes at `arrival`, one at the removed office cutscenes resumes at `officeDesk`, and the record ids `arrest-ledger` and `arrest-word` read back as `board` and `word`.

Checkpoints restart their current story beat. They do not promise frame-exact mid-animation restoration. Changes to save structure must include an explicit migration or graceful rejection.

## Verification

The native Node test runner checks persistence, preview isolation, menu pause behavior, selected story branches and initial-render fidelity. The rendering harness runs the same generated browser code against a small DOM/canvas substitute. It does not emulate a full browser layout or accessibility tree.

## Next separation

Move the original street, station, pump room, roof, club, road, undercity and canal beats into the same registry incrementally, with the current case as the compatibility check.
