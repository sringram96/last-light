# Producer's decisions and build plan

The four design documents (`BEATS.md`, `CHARACTERS.md`, `LOCATIONS.md`, `TECH.md`) were written in parallel against `BRIEF.md`. This page reconciles them where they differ and says what gets built, in what order. Where this page and another document differ, this page wins.

## Reconciliations

1. **The figure above Vale is Halden Ashe, Commissioner of Reserve, Lumen Board.** The beat sheet builds the mystery on the initials H.A. (the ledger's countersignature, the manifest, the dawn deduction), so the Character Director's "Director Maren Coyle" becomes Ashe. The grey coat, the umbrella that never closes, the lit lumen pin and the three lines all carry over; the sprite key `coyle` is renamed `ashe`. Ashe is seen once: standing dry on the dock at Substation Nine in the entry crane, and gone by the manifest beat. He is not arrested tonight; that is the hook for the next case.
2. **Names from the cast bible are used everywhere the beat sheet said "the medic" or "the bridge operator".** Ines Okafor, city medic (green, white cross). Heddy Lasko, Lift Bridge Two, voice only, call sign HALF HOUR; she, not he. Delphine Arlo sings at The Filament and is set dressing this case (her informant beat is optional extra 4). Marta Quill keeps the stall.
3. **Phase ids.** The street keeps its existing ids (`brief`, `watch`, `ready`, `follow`, `danger`, `qte`, `result`, `evidence`, `deduce`, `arrival`) and gains `loftTurn`. The old `ending` phase is removed; a checkpoint saved at `ending` resumes at `arrival`. Every other set uses the beat sheet's ids.
4. **Records migration.** Ending ids `arrest-ledger` and `arrest-word` become `board` and `word` when records are read; `home`, `paper`, `dark` keep their ids; `krane` is new. Discoveries keep their ids.
5. **The tram is built as the beat sheet's quiet bridge**, no prompt. The ninth prompt (the gantry over the tram roof) is optional extra 1 and is not in this build.
6. **The loft deduction costs a rewind on the first wrong answer**, as the beat sheet says. It is the one place a life is lost outside a prompt; if playtests find it harsh, the fallback is `misread` alone.
7. **Materials.** Four new: `cell`, `dawn`, `map`, `arc`, plus the Art Director's change 1 (lamp pools on `tiles`, `roof` and `water`) and change 2 (labels drawn in front of their surface). The `car()` helper gains a `dark` flag (no lamps or tail lights) and a freight body.
8. **Sprites.** The Technical Director's system is in. The sheet thresholds in `CHARACTERS.md` (full from 9 rows) are superseded by `TECH.md` (full from 11); no sheet changes are needed. Bell's `sit` pose is used on the pump walkway and in the interrogation room.
9. **The interrogation room's evidence labels** are `LEDGER`, `MANIFEST`, `CHIP`, `ORDER 7731`, as the Art Director decided.
10. **Roof entry at phone width** hides Rook behind the stair housing for six seconds (Technical Director's finding). Rook's entry path moves to x -6, so he walks clear of the housing from the second second.

## What gets built

Sets, in the order of play: office (extended with the corridor and the board beat), street (per-frame props: the red car, the gutter lantern, the depot stair), **loft** (new), station, pump, roof (the westward view), **tram** (new), **market** (new), club, chase (the substation across the basin), tunnel (the basin opening), **substation** (new), **room** (new), canal (the dawn and the lamps going out).

Story: the full beat sheet, all six endings, all twenty discoveries, the persons-of-interest table, the route table, the eight prompts with their window modifiers, the three deductions, the sixteen transition lines.

## How it gets built

1. **Pipeline first (producer).** A set registry so a set is one file under `src/game/sets/` (build, start shot, shots per phase, blocking, per-frame geometry, labels, exit point, chapter text) and a phase registry so a beat is data (kind, duration, caption, buttons, fields, clue, next, stinger, rewind). The existing case keeps working through the current code while new sets and phases go through the registries. New materials, the save fields, the records migration, the endings, discoveries, persons and route tables, and the flow rewiring (roof to tram, club and chase to the substation, the room before the canal, the loft branch) go in at the same time, by one hand, so the spine is consistent.
2. **Sets in parallel (five agents).** Loft, tram, market, substation, room: each agent builds its module from `LOCATIONS.md` and types its phases from `BEATS.md`, then drives the set in Chromium at 1024 and 375 wide and checks the frames against the density rules before calling it done.
3. **Existing sets (one agent, in parallel with 2).** The Art Director's change list for the nine built sets, in its priority order.
4. **Integration (producer).** Full-case runs on every route in the harness and in Chromium, the reference check, phone frames of every prompt, docs and the README.

# Round two: the game under the picture

The studio head played the shipped case and said: the whole screen should be the game; the text leaves too fast; the action should be something you do on the picture; the game is more forgiving than Dragon's Lair and nothing is at stake. Five reviews under `docs/design/review/` (`GAME.md`, `PRESENTATION.md`, `STAKES.md`, `PLAYTEST.md`, `TECH-2.md`) diagnosed it with numbers: the seven prompts are live for 11 s of a 285 s route; never pressing anything still closes the case in 317 s and earns the `flawless` discovery; 21 of 34 auto-advancing captions cannot be read at 200 wpm; the desktop page scrolls and the phone picture is half the screen; nothing in the picture is clickable. This section reconciles the five and says what gets built. Where a review and this section differ, this section wins.

## Decisions

1. **The screen.** The stage fills the viewport and never scrolls (`PRESENTATION.md` section 2: fixed stage, CSS grid `top | picture | hud`, fixed-height HUD rows, a permanent card slot so cards never move the buttons, the case file and settings in a drawer that pauses the game, a `[FULL SCREEN]` button and the `f` key). The picture fills its area by the Technical Director's vertical field-of-view rule (`TECH-2.md` section 1): 70 rows always, cell size from the height, columns from the width up to 240, `fy` from 70 rows so the approved 180x70 frame is reproduced exactly and wider screens gain set on both sides. Phone portrait fills the height up to 70 rows; when a cell would be under 3.6 px (phone landscape) the picture keeps today's fixed grid and the HUD moves beside it. The reference test is untouched: the harness has no stage, so `resize()` keeps its old rule there. The AGENTS.md rule is reworded as `PRESENTATION.md` proposes: nothing over the canvas, the HUD beside it, cues the one interface drawn in the scene.
2. **The input.** One directional input per action beat (`GAME.md` section 2). The `[1]`/`[2]` world points become cues: two glyphs of the direction (`<<`, `>>`, `^^`, `vv`) in the landed-move cyan, flashing at 4 Hz and 8 Hz in the last third, at the same world points as today. Arrow keys or WASD, a swipe anywhere on the canvas, or a tap on a cue (hit rectangles from `worldLabel`, padded to 44 px, read from the current frame, per `TECH-2.md` section 2). During a timed beat there is no caption, no countdown and no button; the status line reads `LIVE`. The `1` and `2` keys do nothing in timed play. A wrong direction is the beat's miss, at once. A tap outside both cues is ignored. Untimed mode (the accessibility path, default under reduced motion) keeps steady cues with their numbers, the caption and the two buttons, and ignores wrong directions.
3. **Directions per beat** are fixed so the sequence can be learned: courier `^^` catch, `vv` book; inlet `<<` wheel, `>>` Bell; cart `<<` gap, `^^` rope; bottle `vv` duck, `^^` vault; carrier `vv` brake, `>>` dive; bridge `<<` ramp, `^^` jump; fork `>>` right, `<<` left; rack `<<` dive, `>>` breaker.
4. **Windows** measured from the first flash: courier 3.0 s, inlet 2.5, cart 2.5, bottle 2.0, carrier 2.0, bridge 2.0, fork 1.75, rack 1.5; +0.5 s for the earlier observations (`watched`, `decoded`, `tail`, `radio`, `keeper`, `market==='cut'`), -0.5 s for the earlier injuries (`wrong`, `misread`, `shown`, a survived miss); clamp 1.25..3.5. The windup phase keeps its camera cut, loses the `GET READY` card, carries at most ten words shown at once and ending `Get ready.`, and lasts 1.5 to 2.5 s jittered from the checkpoint's `t`. Prompts cap `dt` at 0.05 s and `react()` uses the event timestamp (`TECH-2.md` section 3).
5. **Lamps.** Three per case, the `rewinds` field reused, shown as `LAMPS ###` in the header. A lamp is spent by a death, by choosing to rewind a survivable miss, and by the second wrong answer of a deduction.
6. **Lethal and survivable** (`STAKES.md` section 1, the rule: lethal where the drawn danger would kill and the story has nothing worse to say than death; survivable where the story after is worse than either right answer). Lethal: the inlet (Bell drowns), the cart, the carrier, the jump over the gap with a gap, the fork left to time out, the rack. A death is a `death` phase: the picture from `STAKES.md` section 2, its stinger, 4 s, no buttons, a lamp gone, the night rewinds itself to the windup, whose caption on that pass is the rewind line. Survivable: the courier (the red car takes Nell), the bottle (Krane takes the ledger), the bridge timed out (Vale gone for good); those play their worse story and offer `[REWIND THE NIGHT / n LAMPS]` and `[CARRY ON]`. A wrong direction at a survivable beat is that miss. The two wrong branches at the fork stay choices with soft outcomes; `right` catches only at `gap === 0`.
7. **Case cold.** A death with no lamp left plays its picture, then the `CASE COLD` card and the `coldCase` phase with the six morning-after captions from `STAKES.md`. Records get the seventh ending `cold`, a `cold` count and the deaths list. Buttons: `[RESTART THE CHAPTER]` (three lamps, `restarts` +1, the current set's entry phase, only the fields that set writes reset: never a field earned in an earlier set) and `[RETURN TO MENU]`. There is no limp-on; the `late` values stay valid in the enums for old checkpoints and nothing new writes them.
8. **Deaths seen** are collected: `deaths` is a bitmask (pump 1, market 2, carrier 4, gap 8, pier 16, rack 32), never cleared by a rewind, written to records as `DEATHS SEEN n/6` with the six labels from `STAKES.md`.
9. **Score.** The tally is faced, landed, deaths and restarts; grade A no deaths, B one or two, C otherwise. `flawless` requires no deaths and no restarts. `sharp` adds `!stalled`. Two discoveries join: `stayArrest` and `booth`. Twenty-two discoveries, seven endings; `cold` is evaluated first and `board` before `home`.
10. **Deductions bite** (`STAKES.md` section 3): the first wrong answer costs what the fiction says, the second costs a lamp (or, with Vale or Krane in the chair, stalls the interview: `stalled`, `[WRITE THE WARRANT]`, `board` lost), and the last wrong button is removed.
11. **Antagonists earlier** (`STAKES.md` sections 4 and 5, all on existing cameras): `loftStair` (Vale under the window, `faced`), `clubBooth` (show him order 7731, `shown`, -0.5 s at the bottle), `subDock` (Ashe seen and heard, one line), Krane's confession under the rack, Heddy's log line on the tram, and `roomVale` on the stay route (Vale walks in on Bell; with the dry ledger Rook arrests him in his own building, so `board` is reachable without the chase).
12. **Text** (`PRESENTATION.md` section 3): captions are split at sentence boundaries into chunks of at most 150 characters; typing is 45 characters per second; a chunk holds at least its typing time plus 0.3 s per word plus 0.4 s; a cutscene or result holds at least until its chunks are read; a tap on the picture or the caption, Space or Enter completes the chunk or shows the next one and never skips a beat; the transition line is chunk zero of the next phase, so it survives the dissolve and the card. Windups carry at most ten words; the description moves one beat earlier. The long captions in the review's table are trimmed to thirty words. Reduced motion shows chunks whole with the reading hold.
13. **Survival beats** (`GAME.md` section 3, four single-input beats) are a later round, after a playtest of the eight forks with deaths.
14. **Fields.** New in the checkpoint: `deaths` integer 0..63, `restarts` integer 0..9, `dead` enum (`''`, `pump`, `market`, `carrier`, `gap`, `pier`, `rack`), `stalled`, `faced`, `shown` booleans. Records: `cold` integer, `deaths` list. Nothing else changes shape; absent keys default.

## How it gets built

1. **Mechanics (one builder, the critical path):** the cue phase kind and directions, `promptInput(dir)`, the window curve, keys, swipe and tap, cue drawing and hit rectangles, lamps, the generic `death` kind with the existing late pictures as placeholders, `coldCase`, restart, the save fields, the tally and records, the deduction ladder, tests.
2. **Presentation (one builder, in parallel):** the stage, the fill rule, the HUD rows and card slot, the drawer, fullscreen, the caption queue and holds, the transition line as chunk zero, tests. Region ownership: `resize()`, a new `layout()`, `shell.html`, `build.mjs` and the caption code in `presentation.js` are the presentation builder's; everything else in `runtime.js`, `case.js` and `presentation.js` is the mechanics builder's.
3. **Content (five builders, after 1 lands):** the death pictures and survivable-miss pictures per set; the antagonist beats; `roomVale`; the caption trims and windup lines; `coldCase` captions and the cast-file lines.
4. **Integration (producer):** every route in Chromium at desktop and phone width, the reference check, the AGENTS.md and art-direction rewording, the beat sheet's tables, README, a draft PR.

# Round three: the station as an investigation

An outside review of the first playable build said the case was an interactive short film: clues handed out on arrival, looks that cost nothing, preparation worth half a second, and deductions that are quizzes with a retry. Much of it had been addressed by rounds one and two; what still held was that the look-arounds were free, that the tape bought time rather than knowledge, and that no deduction asked the player to commit to a theory and live with it. This round makes North Station the vertical slice for that loop: observe under pressure, commit to a theory, and pay for it in the action beat.

1. **Turns.** An `investigate` beat may carry `turns`. The maintenance desk has three: five spots, three first looks, the knocking slowing after each (`pressure()`), the title counting down, and the unexamined spots closing once the looks are spent. The office desk and Pump Room 4 stay untimed.
2. **The tape is read at the desk.** Its spot sets `decoded` and gives the inlet clue; the eight-second `stationListen` wait is gone from the flow (`stationQuiet`, `stationListen` and `stationReady` stay registered so old checkpoints resume). At the inlet, a Rook who read it sees the wheel's cue flash alone for the first 0.6 s before Bell's comes up; without it both flash together. The +0.5 s window bonus stays.
3. **The order is needed at the booth.** `[SHOW HIM ORDER 7731]` is offered only when Rook read the order at the desk (or on Bell's map in the loft).
4. **A theory at the hatch** (`stationTheory`, saved as `theory`). Who locked Bell under the station? Names are offered only when the evidence supports them: Vale from the order, the Division padlock or the case board; the courier from the forged call on the office log or Nell's note; the Board from the order's stamp or the departures board. `[NO THEORY]` is always there.
5. **What it costs.** Vale is right and costs nothing. Every other answer is a minute at the hatch: the water is higher, Bell says so, and the inlet window loses 0.5 s. Suspecting the courier costs more: with Nell there, she hears the accusation and will not confess on the roof; with her gone, Rook's call for her fills the Division band, so the bridge operator never gets through and the pursuit runs without the radio's half second. A wrong theory also costs the `sharp` discovery. Bell's line at the platform answers the theory either way.
6. **Not built, on purpose.** Evidence records with reliability and implications, an evidence-picking board, timestamp-based idle jobs and a reorganised checkpoint were proposed; the bitmasks already are evidence by id, and background tabs stop the clock by rule. If the slice plays well, the same three tools (turns, gated cues, a committed theory) go into the loft, substation and booth beats already designed in `INVESTIGATION.md`.
